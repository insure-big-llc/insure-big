It depends on your business. For many small businesses, general liability costs somewhere between a few hundred and a few thousand dollars a year, and the exact figure comes from what you do, how big you are, and the limits you choose. A fair price needs your details, so here is what moves it.

## What general liability covers

General liability pays when someone else says your business caused them harm. The common examples are a customer injured at your location, damage you cause to a client's property, and certain claims about advertising or reputation. It does not cover your own property, your employees' injuries or your vehicles, which have their own policies.

## What decides the price

- **What you do.** A consultant and a roofing contractor pay very different rates, because the chance and size of a claim differ.
- **Your size.** Carriers look at revenue, payroll or both, depending on the type of business.
- **Where you work.** Your location, and whether you work at customers' sites.
- **Your limits.** The most common starting point is $1 million per occurrence and $2 million in total per year. Higher limits cost more.
- **Your claims history.** A clean record helps.
- **Your deductible,** if the policy has one.

## Why the limit may be set for you

Landlords, general contractors and customers often require a certificate showing $1 million per occurrence before they let you work or lease space. Check what your contract or lease asks for before you buy, so you do not pay for cover you cannot use.

## A business owners policy may cost less

If you also need property cover, a business owners policy (BOP) bundles general liability with commercial property, and is often priced lower than buying the two separately. We compare both ways.

## What to have ready for a quote

- A clear description of what the business does, and any work done at customers' locations.
- Annual revenue and payroll.
- The number of employees.
- Any contract or lease requirements for limits.
- Your current policy and recent claims, if you have them.

## Questions we have been asked

**Does general liability cover my employees if they are hurt?**

No. Employee injuries fall under workers' compensation. In Missouri, most employers with five or more employees must carry it.

Many homeowners policies in the Kansas City area use a separate deductible for wind and hail, and it is often a percentage of your home's insured value rather than a flat dollar amount. On a larger home that can be a big number on the day you file a claim. Here is how it works and how to compare.

## What a percentage deductible means

Instead of a deductible such as $1,000, the policy says you pay a percentage of your dwelling coverage before the insurer pays for wind or hail damage. The percentage is often 1%, 2% or 5%, depending on the carrier and your roof.

For a home insured for $300,000:

| Wind and hail deductible | What you pay first |
|---|---|
| 1% | $3,000 |
| 2% | $6,000 |
| 5% | $15,000 |

Your deductible for other claims, such as a kitchen fire, can still be a flat amount. The two are separate.

## Why this matters in Kansas City

The Kansas City metro is exposed to hail, and the insurance market has responded with stricter roof rules and percentage deductibles. A lower premium can hide a high wind and hail deductible, so the cheaper quote can be the more expensive claim.

## What to check on any quote

1. **The wind and hail deductible,** as a percentage and as a dollar amount.
2. **How the roof is paid.** Some policies pay the roof's full replacement cost. Others pay its depreciated value, which can be far less on an older roof.
3. **Cosmetic damage terms.** Some policies will not pay to replace a roof that still works but looks marked by hail.
4. **Roof age rules.** Some carriers limit cover or raise the price as a roof ages.

## If a carrier has restricted your roof

After a claim or two, or as a roof gets older, a standard carrier may raise the deductible, limit roof cover or decline to renew. A specialist wind and hail market may be an option. ## How to compare quotes

- Ask for the same dwelling amount and the same deductibles on every quote.
- Put the wind and hail deductible next to the premium, in dollars.
- Read the roof payment and cosmetic damage terms before you decide.

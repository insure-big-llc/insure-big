In Missouri, most employers with five or more employees must carry workers' compensation insurance, and construction employers must carry it with even one employee. There are exceptions, and the rules can change, so confirm your own situation. Here is what the cover does and how to get it right.

## What workers' compensation does

When an employee is hurt on the job or becomes ill because of their work, workers' compensation pays for medical treatment and part of the lost wages. In most cases it also protects the employer from being sued by that employee over the injury.

## The Missouri rule in plain words

- **Five or more employees:** most employers must carry workers' compensation.
- **Construction:** employers in the construction industry must carry it even with one employee.
- **Under five employees:** it may not be required by law, but many businesses carry it anyway. A single serious injury can cost more than a year of premiums, and some customers and general contractors will not work with you without it.

The details, such as how owners, family members, subcontractors and some agricultural work are counted, have exceptions. Check with the Missouri Division of Workers' Compensation or ask us. We would rather confirm than guess.

## What decides the price

- **Payroll.** Premiums are based on how much you pay your employees.
- **Job classification.** Each type of work has a classification code, and riskier work costs more.
- **Your claims history.** Fewer claims over time lowers what you pay.
- **Safety practices.** Carriers look at how you prevent injuries.

## What to have ready for a quote

- The number of employees and what each one does.
- Your payroll for the last year and what you expect this year.
- Your current policy, if you have one, and any recent claims.
- Whether you use subcontractors, and whether they carry their own cover.
